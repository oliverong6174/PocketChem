## 2. Forces and Newton's laws (`utils/physics/forces.ts`, `ForcesPage.tsx`, `ForceDiagram.tsx`)
One body on a flat or inclined surface (0–80°) with gravity, normal force, an applied force at any angle to the surface (labelled push/pull or rope tension), and static or kinetic friction; or a block on a table joined over an ideal pulley to a hanging mass (both reduce to one 1-D friction engine). Normal force is N = mg cosθ − F sinφ, never assumed mg; if that is negative the body has left the surface and N = friction = 0 (only weight and the applied force are reported, and the landing is not modelled). Static friction adapts up to μₛN (equal to the net other force, not μₛN); kinetic friction is μₖN against the sliding. Motion is analytic and piecewise: a decelerating slide ends when it stops, then the forces decide whether it stays (static) or slides the other way (force reversal, e.g. up a slope and back). Impending motion (push exactly μₛN) is flagged "on the verge". Constant velocity with zero net force is reported as such. Interactive free-body diagram: all arrows on one scale from the same moment as the readouts, net-force and acceleration arrows, tilted surface, the body sliding with the simulation time, force table with components along/perpendicular to the surface, clickable arrows/rows with an inspector (e.g. mg splits into mg sinθ and mg cosθ).

## 3. Work and mechanical energy (`energy.ts`, `EnergyPage.tsx`, `EnergyDiagram.tsx`)
Four analytic, time-based scenarios: block on an incline (reference height = bottom, start anywhere on it, launch up or down), rough horizontal surface with an optional constant applied force, spring launch (ideal Hookean spring, SHM about the friction-shifted equilibrium, leaves at the natural length or stops still compressed, held by static friction if kx₀ ≤ μₛmg, then slides free), and an object changing height (user-chosen PE reference height). One ledger at every instant: KE + PE_g + PE_s + Q = E(0) + W_applied (Q = heat from kinetic friction; mechanical energy falls while the total is conserved). Work by gravity, spring, friction and the applied force is reported separately and sums to ΔKE (work–energy theorem). A "Can it get there?" question finds the first time a position is reached and refuses with the energy reason when it is not (kinetic energy would be negative). KE never goes negative; round-off dust (≈1e-15 J) is shown as exactly 0.

## 4. Momentum and collisions (`collisions.ts`, `CollisionsPage.tsx`, `CollisionDiagram.tsx`)
Two rigid 1 m wide bodies on a frictionless line, A left of B, independent masses and velocities, coefficient of restitution 0–1 (elastic, partly elastic, perfectly inelastic). vA′ = (p − mB·e·(vA−vB))/M, vB′ = (p + mA·e·(vA−vB))/M. A collision exists only if vA > vB; separating or equal-speed bodies get "no collision" and nothing is invented. Contact time = gap/closing speed, positions at contact, piecewise constant-velocity motion before and after (the faces touch exactly at t_c). Outputs: momentum before/after, centre-of-mass velocity (unchanged), KE before/after and loss (= ½μ(1−e²)(vA−vB)²), impulse J = Δp (equal and opposite). An average force is shown only if the user supplies a collision duration; no contact-force history is drawn.

## 5. Numerical models and assumptions
Point/rigid bodies, constant g, Coulomb friction with μₖ ≤ μₛ, ideal pulley and string, ideal massless Hookean spring, no air resistance, instantaneous 1-D collisions with constant e. All deterministic closed forms evaluated at the shared time (no integration, so no frame-rate error). Limits are shown on each page ("Assumptions and limits") and in the overview.

## 10. Unsupported physical scenarios
Rolling/rotation/tipping, air drag, more than two bodies, a slack or elastic string, pulleys with mass or friction, curved or moving surfaces, the landing/flight of a body that leaves its surface, springs with mass or damping, non-Hookean springs, a block that runs off the end of a track, collisions in two dimensions or with walls, external forces during a collision, contact-force histories.
