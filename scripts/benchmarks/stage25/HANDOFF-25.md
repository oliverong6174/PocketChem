the same reaction as ketogenesis' first step). Existing Stage 18 metabolites gained only compartments (acetyl-CoA, citrate, α-KG, fumarate, CoA in the cytosol).

## 3. Fatty-acid activation
ACS (long chain at the outer membrane, medium chain in the matrix) → pyrophosphatase → adenylate kinase (accounting). Computed from reactions: 1 ATP molecule,
1 then 2 high-energy bonds, 2 ATP equivalents (any chain length). Separate from the β-oxidation pathway (tested).

## 4. Carnitine shuttle
CPT I (outer membrane, malonyl-CoA target) → translocase (antiport, inner membrane) → CPT II (matrix); step-through diagram with compartment bands. Short/medium
chains (C4–C10) shown without the shuttle (activated in the matrix), C12 flagged borderline, C14+ required (boundary is approximate and stated).

## 5. β-Oxidation
Four reactions per cycle, stepped one reaction at a time (28 steps for palmitate); the chain bar, cumulative NADH/FADH₂/acetyl-CoA chips and totals come from the same
reaction sequence. Even chains C4–C18: cycles n/2−1, acetyl-CoA n/2, NADH = FADH₂ = cycles. Palmitate: 7 cycles, 8 acetyl-CoA, 7 NADH, 7 FADH₂, activation 2,
conventional **106** ATP equivalents (2.5/1.5/10 per carrier/acetyl-CoA; shown as 17.5 + 10.5 + 80 − 2); β-oxidation itself makes 0 ATP. Special cases: odd chain
(exact products incl. propionyl-CoA, PCC/MCEE/MUT with biotin and B₁₂, **no ATP value**); oleate (isomerase skips one FADH₂: 8 cycles, 9 acetyl-CoA, 8 NADH, 7 FADH₂,
118.5). Unsupported and listed: polyunsaturated, very-long, branched, modified fatty acids, C<4, C>18.

## 6. Fatty-acid synthesis
Palmitate: 8 acetyl units, 7 malonyl-CoA, 7 rounds, 14 NADPH, 7 ATP (ACC) + 8 ATP (citrate export, optional source), 44-reaction sequence stepped in the UI, ACP-bound
chain, NADPH from malic enzyme (8, derived) versus other sources (6, labelled not modelled), ATP and NADPH in separate rows, six contrasts explaining why it is not β-oxidation
backward (location, carrier, reductant, D vs L hydroxy isomer, driving force, control).

## 7. Ketogenesis and ketolysis
ACAT → HMG-CoA synthase → HMG-CoA lyase → BDH1 → export (acetone as a side branch); net 2 acetyl-CoA + H₂O → acetoacetate + 2 CoA (+ NADH for β-HB). Ketolysis: uptake →
BDH1 (reverse) → SCOT → thiolase. SCOT is extrahepatic only, HMG-CoA synthase/lyase liver only (tissue tags); tissue table includes red blood cells. Ketone-derived ATP:
acetoacetate 19, β-HB 21.5 (SCOT bypasses one GTP); carbohydrate- vs ketone-derived acetyl-CoA compared upstream of the shared acetyl-CoA identity (+6, −0.5, +0.75).

## 8. Amino-acid catabolism
All 20 amino acids with structure, amino group, skeleton and curated destinations; classification is **derived** from endpoints and matches the standard split
(leucine, lysine ketogenic only; I, F, T, W, Y both; 13 glucogenic). Reactions in full only where stoichiometric (ALT, AST, GDH, GLS, GS, the urea cycle); all
other routes are labelled curated relationships with no invented intermediate enzymes; the nominal-glucogenic caveat is displayed.

## 9. Transamination and deamination
Six selectable substrates (Ala, Asp, Leu, Val, Ile, Tyr) with formulas balanced against Stage 17 data, eight-stage PLP mechanism (amino group on amino acid → PMP → glutamate;
free ammonia never appears), GDH with NAD⁺ or NADP⁺ and either direction, and a computed transamination-vs-deamination table (NH₄⁺ 0 vs +1).

## 10. Urea cycle
Eight steps (CPS I, OTC, citrulline out, ASS, PPase, ASL, arginase, ornithine in) by compartment; nitrogen ledger by atom source (NH₄⁺ via carbamoyl phosphate and aspartate;
carbon from CO₂; checked against formula nitrogen counts at every step); energy: **3 ATP molecules, 4 high-energy bonds** (2 + 1 + 1), per-step table; the fumarate →
aspartate NADH recovery (≈2.5 ATP, estimated net 1.5 bond equivalents) is shown separately; NAG/arginine regulation of CPS I is qualitative; no clinical predictions.

## 11. Carbon, nitrogen and energy accounting
`fuel.ts` gives separate carbon (skeleton carbons, carriers excluded), nitrogen (carrier and CoA nitrogen excluded), ATP/GTP and bonds, NADH/FADH₂ and NADPH ledgers. Every
registered non-transport reaction conserves carbon and nitrogen (tested); a pathway listed twice is added once; NADPH is never merged with NADH; nitrogen disposal consumes ATP.
